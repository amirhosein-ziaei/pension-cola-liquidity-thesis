## Search coverage and limits

- Searches covered the title and subtitle variants; author names with "liquidity", "COLA" and "inflation"; "public pension COLA inflation cash flow asset sales"; NBER/SSRN 2025–26 work on inflation and public pensions; and Wharton PRC 2026 papers.
- **No paper was found that uses COLA rules or the 2021–23 inflation surge as a shock to pension cash flows or asset sales.** This is a bounded search result, not proof of absence. SSRN could not be searched directly (HTTP 403), which is a material gap for a field where working papers live on SSRN.
- Practitioner sources used only for institutional facts: [NASRA COLA brief, June 2026](https://www.nasra.org/files/Issue%20Briefs/NASRACOLABrief.pdf) (FT); [CRR, Aug 2022](https://crr.bc.edu/how-are-public-pensions-faring-with-falling-markets-and-rising-inflation/) (page); [Equable 2023 COLAs](https://equable.org/public-pension-colas/) (page).

## Net assessment

The **economic mechanism** (cash pressure → liquid-asset sales, worse with illiquid portfolios) is occupied. The **only** surviving increment is the identifying variation: predetermined, benefit-side, inflation-triggered COLA cash flows. The thesis is therefore a better-identified re-estimate of a known effect, with worse outcome data than the competitor. It is worth something only if the shock is large enough to estimate the effect more precisely or more credibly than AJR. `identification_risks.md` argues that it is not.
